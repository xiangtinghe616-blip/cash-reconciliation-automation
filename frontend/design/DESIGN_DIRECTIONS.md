# Candidate Design Directions

## Direction 1 — Institutional Operations Desk

A calm, precise, high-trust workbench for finance operations.

Best for:
- portfolio credibility
- audit-aware workflows
- conservative financial users
- clear differentiation from AI dashboard aesthetics

Visual traits:
- deep slate header
- off-white background
- disciplined cards
- sparse accent color
- red and amber only for meaningful risk
- strong typography hierarchy

Risk:
- may feel too conservative if not polished enough

## Direction 2 — Audit-Grade Exception Console

A more control-heavy interface focused on evidence, traceability, and review discipline.

Best for:
- controllers
- audit reviewers
- compliance-sensitive workflows
- explaining why AI does not make final decisions

Visual traits:
- evidence-first layout
- status tags
- audit trail preview
- compact comparison tables
- strong distinction between confirmed, candidate, and action states

Risk:
- may feel heavy or less visually elegant

## Direction 3 — High-Density Cash Break Command Center

A more operationally intense interface designed for high-volume review.

Best for:
- showing throughput improvement
- demonstrating queue-based work
- emphasizing 100 breaks to 1000 breaks narrative

Visual traits:
- high-density priority queue
- keyboard-like workflow feeling
- more compact cards
- status lanes
- batch review affordances

Risk:
- can become cluttered if hierarchy is not carefully controlled

## Current Recommendation

Start with Direction 1 as the base style.

Borrow from Direction 2 for audit trail and evidence structure.

Borrow from Direction 3 for queue efficiency and throughput cues.

Implementation target:

Institutional Operations Desk with Audit-Grade Evidence and Command-Center Queue Discipline.
